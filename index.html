Neuro-Oncology Notes

Practical learning and temozolomide tools for clinicians and trainees.

Cycle datesDose arithmeticToxicity review & grading

Educational prototype for adult treatment. Verify against the treatment plan and institutional protocol before clinical use. These tools do not issue treatment clearance. No patient identifiers are needed; calculator inputs stay in this browser page and are not stored or transmitted.

1. Cycle dates

Current cycle and three potential future cycles, assuming 28-day cycles without delays.

Projections do not determine treatment duration. Each subsequent cycle requires laboratory review and clinical approval. If treatment is delayed, recalculate from the actual new start date. Additional laboratory testing may be required.

2. Dose arithmetic

Use the verified body surface area from the treatment plan.

Daily dose = verified BSA × selected dose level. No BSA cap or capsule rounding is applied. Escalation is not automatic. Confirm the prescribed dose, capsule combination and schedule with the treating team and pharmacy.

3. Toxicity review

Label-based review during concurrent treatment or before a subsequent adjuvant/maintenance cycle. Not for initial clearance, dose escalation, or resuming remaining doses within an interrupted adjuvant cycle.

Nonhematologic toxicity legend — how to choose a grade

Grade the specific adverse event, then enter the highest relevant grade. This general severity guide is not a substitute for event-specific CTCAE criteria.

GradeGeneral meaning
0No relevant toxicity; a tool entry, not a CTCAE adverse-event grade.
1Mild findings, usually with no treatment needed.
2Moderate; may need limited treatment or interfere with tasks such as shopping, cooking or managing finances.
3Severe or medically significant; may warrant hospitalization or interfere with bathing, dressing or feeding oneself.
4Life-threatening; urgent treatment needed.
5Death attributable to the adverse event; not an input for prospective dosing.

For these TEMODAR modification tables, exclude alopecia, nausea and vomiting from the grade entered. Those symptoms still need clinical management. Assess other events individually, including liver abnormalities, fatigue, diarrhea, rash or mucositis; each has its own grading criteria.

ULN = the reporting laboratory's upper limit of normal. Upper endpoints are included: ALT exactly 5× ULN is grade 2; above 5× is grade 3, with a normal baseline.

If baseline was above ULN, do not use this table. Consult the full CTCAE entry for baseline-relative criteria. Use the version required by your institution or trial: v5.0 and v6.0 differ for abnormal baselines. The legend does not automatically assign a grade or establish drug causality. Clinical liver injury may require action beyond an isolated laboratory grade.

Counts must be in ×10⁹/L: ANC 1.5 = 1,500/µL; platelets 100 = 100,000/µL. Use ANC, not total WBC.

Use nadirs and worst grades from the current concurrent course or the adjuvant cycle being reviewed, including the current assessment. Unknown grades or incomplete nadir history prevent a complete assessment. Previous permanent discontinuation, infection, bleeding, suspected liver injury and other clinical concerns can override these limited criteria.
